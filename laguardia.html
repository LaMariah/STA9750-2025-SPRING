The First-Gen Cliff: how a comparative study reshaped a college program

A mixed-methods research project at LaGuardia Community College that quantified why first-generation students leave — and turned the evidence into a launched degree.

Gerusa de Souza · LaGuardia Community College, CUNY · Presented at the SCRA Biennial Conference, June 23, 2023

First-generation students arrive at college without a family map for how it works. We suspected that gap showed up as measurable risk — so we designed a study to find out where, when, and how much.

01The question

LaGuardia's first-year retention (60%) trails the national average, and the loss is not evenly distributed. The NEH Health Humanities initiative needed evidence to design a program that would actually hold first-generation students — not assumptions. My role was to build the research that would answer one question: what specifically puts first-gen students at risk, and when?

02The design — a controlled comparison

I framed it as an A/B comparative study. The control group was second-generation students (at least one parent attended college — they carry a family model). The treatment group was first-generation students, navigating without one. Both groups were scored across seven risk dimensions on a 1–10 scale, so differences could be measured rather than described.

Second-gen ≈ 45% of enrolled students (behavioral baseline) · First-gen ≈ 55% (primary group under study).

03Finding — first-gen students carry 2–3× the strain

Across every dimension, first-generation students scored dramatically higher on risk. Financial stress, mental-health strain, and academic-direction uncertainty were the widest gaps — each a critical difference of five points or more.

04Finding — the dropout cliff is Semester 1

Timing mattered as much as magnitude. First-gen dropout risk peaks at 9.0 in the first semester, then falls steadily as students who survive find their footing. The second-gen line stays flat and low throughout. The implication for program design was unambiguous: intervention has to land early, before the first semester ends.

05Finding — the barriers are compound, and they're material

The student interest survey used a multi-select barrier question by design — single-choice would have undercounted the real, overlapping pressures students face. Work obligations (71%), financial constraints (64%), and unclear career pathways (58%) topped the list for first-gen students, each roughly double the second-gen rate.

06Context — how LaGuardia compares

The student-level gaps sit inside an institutional one. LaGuardia trails national benchmarks on retention and completion — but the CUNY ASAP comparison shows what targeted support does: a 53% outcome against a 24% control, +29 points. Support that's designed around the risk works.

07The qualitative layer — listening before designing

Numbers tell you where; people tell you why. I coded the BRXCRRB community focus-group transcript line by line, extracting four themes that shaped how the program would be built and marketed:

08The outcome

The evidence base went to LaGuardia leadership and was presented at the SCRA Biennial Conference. The headline result isn't a chart — it's a program: the Health Humanities AA degree is now live in the LaGuardia catalog, designed around findings showing where first-gen students need support and when.

Why this is business analysis

The subject was students, but the method is the one a business analyst uses every day: define the question, design the instrument, run a controlled comparison, code the qualitative signal, and hand stakeholders something they can act on. Survey design becomes A/B variable definition; focus-group coding becomes voice-of-customer analysis; the leadership dashboard becomes KPI reporting.

"The challenge changes — students or customers, college or telecom. The work is the same: understand what the audience needs, design the instrument to find out, analyze with rigor, and give the business something it can act on."
